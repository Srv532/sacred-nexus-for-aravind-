Night draped over Devapuram like a velvet shroud, studded with the diamond-sharp points of distant stars. Below, the crystalline towers of the palace ceased their daytime blazing and settled into a soft, internal luminescence, their glow reflecting faintly in the polished marble of the grand courtyards. The air grew cool, carrying the scent of night-blooming jasmine from the royal gardens. Along the outer walls, the rhythmic tread of guards making their rounds was a counterpoint to the chirping of insects in the groves beyond. The day's revelations had left a palpable tension in the air, a coiled silence that even the evening breeze seemed hesitant to disturb.

Devaj, his face carved from shadow and torchlight, had ordered the palace guard to double their patrols. He stood with Aadhyan and his senior commanders, his voice low and steady as he issued precise, careful instructions.

Nearby, Anshul lounged against a pillar, surrounded by his own coterie of black-armored loyalists. An ever-present smirk played on his lips. "Such caution is unbecoming of a prince," he declared, his voice loud enough to carry. He pushed himself off the pillar and made a grand, sweeping gesture toward the darkened forests beyond the city walls. "My men and I will patrol the outer districts. Perhaps we'll indulge in a hunt. They say the forest beasts grow bold on nights like these. It could prove... entertaining."

Devaj's eyes narrowed to slits of cold fury. "The security of the palace and its immediate perimeter is our only priority. There will be no reckless excursions. That is an order."

Anshul let out a short, barking laugh that was devoid of all humor. "Orders? You still hide behind rules and procedures, cousin, like a frightened child hiding behind his mother's robes. You lack the courage to act, the strength to rule. You always have." He took a menacing step forward, his gaze flicking past Devaj to land on Aadhyan. His smirk twisted into a sneer of pure contempt. "You surround yourself with relics and ghosts. You, and that scum impostor…"

The air didn't just turn cold; it froze. The ambient sounds of the night—the wind, the insects, the distant city—seemed to die in an instant. Every soldier, every courtier within earshot, went utterly still. Aadhyan felt the shift, a sudden, violent drop in atmospheric pressure, as if a storm had materialized out of nowhere.

"Scum impostor?" Aadhyan repeated, his voice dangerously level, a commander's demand for clarification.

Before Anshul could spew more venom, a voice like the crack of a whip sliced through the silence from the upper balcony.

"Do not speak his name!"

Queen Aarini stood above them, a figure of incandescent rage. Her knuckles were white where she gripped the balustrade, her face a mask of such profound hatred that it was terrifying to behold.

"Don't you dare speak of that disgusting essence of hell in my presence!" her voice thundered, echoing across the courtyard, a raw, uncontrolled sound that shattered her carefully constructed regal facade.

The aftermath was a study in contained devastation. Aadhyan, the scientist, observed the chain reaction. Devaj stood rigid, his face impassive, but his eyes were fixed on a distant point, seeing nothing of the present. He looked like a man drowning in a memory he could not escape. Beside him, Tanisha's hand flew to her mouth, her sharp, strategic mind momentarily lost to stunned concern for the man she loved. Ashoka was visibly trembling, his hand resting on the hilt of his sword, while Akash had gone pale, his mouth opening and closing with no sound coming out.

But it was Aaradhya who held Aadhyan's attention. She stood unshaken, her posture a column of defiant strength. Yet her face, though outwardly composed, was a portrait of quiet, shattering grief. Her eyes shimmered with unshed tears, and her lips trembled with the effort of holding back a thousand unspoken words. She wasn't just sad; she was mourning, freshly and deeply, a wound torn open anew.

The silence stretched, thick and suffocating. Finally, Anshul scoffed, breaking the spell. With a final, contemptuous glare, he turned and melted into the shadows with his men, their laughter a parting insult that lingered in the heavy air.

Later, in the opulent guest chamber assigned to him, Aadhyan lay on a vast, silk-draped bed. Above him, the ceiling was a masterful illusion, a celestial dome depicting the constellations of ancient Deva legends in glittering, magical light. But his mind was a storm, unable to find peace in the painted serenity. The unspoken name, the fury, the grief—it was another piece of the puzzle, a piece connected intrinsically to the hidden statue, to the very rottenness at the heart of the empire.

Sleep, when it finally came, was not a reprieve. It was a plunge into the abyss of another's memory.

He was Dr. Sanjay again, standing in the familiar, sterile environment of his lab. The air hummed with power and smelled of ozone. Beside him, Dr. Atharv—younger, more frantic—was shouting warnings. "The energy cascade is unstable, Sanjay! The feedback loop is critical! Shut it down!" But he hadn't listened, driven by a reckless hunger for discovery. Then, the world dissolved into an explosion of blinding, silent, white light.

The vision fractured, reforming into something else entirely. He was no longer in the lab. He stood in a void, a space between worlds. Before him was a warrior. The man's face was hauntingly familiar, like looking at a distorted reflection of his own, yet different—older, scarred, and burdened by a sorrow that seemed as ancient as the stars. He wore ornate bronze armor, intricately embroidered with patterns that pulsed with a faint, inner light.

"I failed," the warrior said, his voice a gravelly echo laced with an eternity of regret. "I could not stop the decay. But my failure has created this one, singular chance." He looked at Sanjay, his eyes pleading. "I do not fully understand the mechanics of this transference, but it seems our very existence, our worlds, are intertwined. This must be a divine gambit… a desperate measure to save us all. I am giving you everything. My consciousness, my skills, my memories. I do not know how much of you will remain, or how much of me you can bear. But it must be done."

The warrior reached forward, and his right index finger, which glowed with the intensity of a miniature sun, pressed gently against Sanjay's forehead.

A flash. An agony. A torrent.

He was inundated. Not with images, but with raw sensation. The scent of lotus blossoms in a sun-drenched royal garden. The searing heat of a celestial forge. The terrified screams of a city on fire. The taste of blood and ash. The unyielding cold of a throne. The heartbreaking sound of a child crying a name. A battle cry that tore from his own throat. Faces flashed through the chaos—Devaj, Aaradhya, a benevolent Queen Devanshi, and another boy, his face always blurry, just out of focus.

Sanjay's body convulsed in the dream, a silent scream trapped in his throat. He jolted awake, gasping, the celestial constellations of the chamber ceiling swimming in his vision. His forehead tingled, an phantom heat lingering where the warrior's finger had touched him.

A gentle knock preceded Akash's entry. "Commander? The War Council is in emergency session. Prince Devaj requests your presence."

Aadhyan sat up, rubbing his forehead, the impossible memories still clinging to him like static. "I'll be ready."

The grand council chamber was alive with hushed, anxious energy. Generals and commanders stood in tight clusters around the holographic map, which now showed alarming crimson alerts pulsing along the western border. Devaj presided from the head of the table, his face calm but his eyes betraying a sleepless night.

A young general was giving a report. "My lord, the situation is… inexplicable. Our forward patrols report that the Rakshasa troops have simply vanished. No signs of battle, no bodies, no retreat tracks. It's as if the earth swallowed them whole."

"A tactical retreat to lure us into a trap," one of the older, more cynical commanders grumbled.

"Or perhaps the work of the Shuddha Maanav," another suggested. "An internal attack on our enemies could be seen as a boon."

Before the debate could escalate, a new crisis arrived. A messenger from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs swept into the room, his face pale with urgency. "A formal declaration from the Asurya Empire, delivered this morning!"

A palpable tension seized the room. The Head of the Ministry, a stern, gray-haired man, stepped forward and unrolled a scroll that bore the menacing imperial seal of Emperor Durjaya.

He read, his voice ringing with grim finality: "Let it be known that the Amarakarsh Empire stands accused of piracy and aggression against the sovereign trade of the Asurya Empire. Our merchant vessels have been unlawfully seized, our trade partners intimidated, and our economic stability threatened. This campaign of interference will cease immediately. Any further disruption will be met with the full and undivided might of the Asurya legions. Consider this a final warning."

The room erupted. "Absurd!" one councilor roared. "He manufactures a reason for war!"

As if the news weren't dire enough, the Head of the Crime Control Department stepped forward next. "My lords, our intelligence confirms what we have long suspected. Emperor Durjaya's influence within our borders is growing. Networks of human trafficking, drug smuggling, and illegal arms sales have been traced back to Asurya. Worse," he paused, letting the weight of his next words settle, "we have confirmed the existence of a high-level spy, operating within this very palace. We believe they have been active for years."

The murmurs turned to outright suspicion, with councilors and generals exchanging distrustful glances. It was into this cauldron of paranoia and external threat that a new presence arrived, his entry commanding immediate silence and respect.

Dhrishtdev, the Prime Minister of the Amarakarsh Empire, strode into the chamber. He was a man in his prime, with a sharp, intelligent face and eyes that missed nothing. He bowed formally, first to Devaj, then with a pointed coolness to Aarini, who sat observing the proceedings like a hawk.

"My lady, my lords," Dhrishtdev began, his voice calm but resonant with authority. "A foreign threat looms, and our internal stability is compromised. This is a time for strategy, not for squabbling. We must strengthen our counter-intelligence and reopen diplomatic channels, however distasteful."

Anshul, who had been preening near his mother, cut in with a sneer. "The Empire is strong. We do not need to bow to outsiders."

Dhrishtdev's eyes flashed with cold fire. "The Empire needs decisive leadership, not the reckless pride of a boy who speaks as if he wears the crown."

A collective gasp went through the chamber. Aadhyan watched, a flicker of admiration for the Prime Minister's courage igniting within him.

"The Empire thrived," Dhrishtdev continued, his voice like steel, "under the wise rule of Queen Devanshi and her two rightful princes. Not under a self-proclaimed regent or her bratty, un-blooded son."

Two rightful princes? Aadhyan's mind seized on the phrase. Devaj is one. But who is the other? The blurry boy from my dream?

Aarini slammed her palm on the stone table, the sound cracking through the chamber like a gunshot. "How dare you! How dare you mention that traitor in this sacred hall!"

"I speak only the truth, as is my duty," Dhrishtdev replied, unfazed. "The Queen Mother still lives. Her silence does not grant you her throne. Anshul was never in the line of succession, and every person in this room knows why."

Aarini rose from her seat, her face contorted with a fury that seemed to momentarily age her. "You speak with such confidence! As if you believe her health will miraculously recover, as if some cursed spell will simply be broken now that your—" She stopped abruptly, her eyes widening as she realized what she had said.

The air in the chamber became unnaturally still. Cursed spell. The words hung in the silence, a confession of a truth far darker than mere political maneuvering. Dhrishtdev's face darkened, his expression confirming he had heard exactly what he needed to. The session was adjourned moments later, the council dissolving in a state of chaos and suspicion.

Aadhyan scanned the room as everyone began to file out. Aaradhya was gone. He found her at the southern gate, no longer in her courtly robes but clad in practical, polished armor, a longsword strapped to her back. Her personal troops were making final preparations for departure.

"Aaradhya?" he called out.

She turned, a flicker of surprise in her eyes. "Where are you going?" he asked.

Before she could answer, Anshul strode toward them, his presence oozing malice. "Look! The adopted princess is gearing up for another one of her pathetic, little quests. Still chasing ghosts, are you? Still clinging to the foolish rumor that he's alive somewhere out there?" His grin was a twisted, ugly thing. "You really are a naive idiot. The Queen Mother only made you my 'sister' to keep you out of my reach, but don't think that title will protect you forever, you little bi—"

"Anshul!" Akash's voice cut through the venom like a blade. "The Weapon Regulation Department requires your immediate presence. An audit of your personal guard's armaments."

Anshul shot Akash a glare filled with hatred, then scoffed and stalked away. Aaradhya remained silent, but Aadhyan could see her hands were trembling.

"She protects the outer villages," Akash explained quietly to Aadhyan. "Places that are frequent targets for Rakshasa remnants and the Shuddha Maanav. She was raised in one of them, before… before the Queen adopted her."

Aadhyan looked at Aaradhya, at the grief and determination warring in her eyes, and a decision solidified within him. The palace was a cage of whispers and lies. The truth was out there, in the world he was supposed to protect.

"I'm coming with you," he said.

She stared at him, startled. "Why?"

"Because my memories are of the battlefield, not the court," he replied, his voice firm. "And I need to know what I am fighting for."

Back in his chamber, he went to a heavy, ornate golden chest. He knelt and felt along the bottom edge, finding a hidden seam. A section of the floor slid away, revealing a concealed compartment. Inside, nestled in worn velvet, lay a weapon—a single-edged blade, somewhere between a short sword and a long knife, its hilt wrapped in dark, worn leather. His old weapon. The moment his palm closed around the handle, a circuit completed. A familiar, humming heat flowed from the metal into his hand, and faint, blue sparks danced along the blade's edge.

And with the heat, came the memories. Not a torrent this time, but clear, sharp fragments. Combat stances. Divine strikes. The roar of a command. And then… a small boy with Devaj's eyes, laughing as Aadhyan lifted him onto his shoulders. And beside him, another boy, the blurry one, smiling.

Who were you? The question burned in his mind. Why does the memory of you feel like a phantom limb?

He stood, clasping the weapon, his reflection in the polished blade showing a man with the eyes of a scientist and the soul of a warrior. A journey was beginning—not just across a troubled empire, but deep into the treacherous, war-torn landscape of his own past.
