"Long live the Prince!" Ashoka's voice was a reverent roar beside him, lost in the din of battle.

From Sanjay's vantage point, the man who wore Atharv's face was a force of nature. Devaj moved with an impossible fluidity, his twin blades a blur of golden light. He didn't just fight; he danced, a whirlwind of controlled destruction. Sanjay's scientific mind struggled to comprehend it. The energy that Devaj channeled from his own body, bursting outwards to shatter enemy lines, defied every known law of physics. It was beautiful. It was terrifying.

"The commander's lost his senses!" Akash yelled to Devaj over the chaos, recounting the blow to Aadhyan's head. "I don't think he even remembers his own name!"

Devaj's gaze flickered towards them, his expression unreadable for a moment. "Ashoka, you have command of the formation! Akash, get the Commander to safety. Use the sky-barge. Now!"

"This way, old man!" Akash grabbed Sanjay's armored arm and pulled him toward a sleek, metallic craft hovering silently a few feet off the blood-soaked ground. The carriage, as Akash called it, was forged from the same light-swallowing alloy as the prototype in his lab. A shiver that had nothing to do with the cold ran down Sanjay's spine.

The door slid open with a faint hiss, revealing an interior that was a bizarre fusion of luxury and military function. Plush seating was bolted next to racks of strange, pistol-like weapons. As they lifted into the air, the roar of the battle muted to a dull thrum. The barge accelerated with a force that pressed Sanjay deep into his seat.

Below, the battlefield shrank into a tapestry of fire and chaos. Sanjay watched Devaj, a lone golden figure, hold back a tide of snarling Rakshasa. He wore golden armor over simple black trousers and a white tunic, a stark contrast of the ancient and the modern.

"His unique power," Akash said, noticing Sanjay's rapt attention. "The ability to manifest his inner energy. No other in the Deva clan can do it with such raw power."

Just as Akash spoke, a violent lurch threw them against their seats. A deep, groaning screech echoed from the roof of the barge.

"What was that?" Sanjay asked.

"Get down!" Akash shouted, fumbling for one of the weapons on the rack. "Something's on the hull!"

A section of the ceiling ripped inwards with a scream of tearing metal. A hulking Rakshasa dropped inside, its eyes burning with red-hot malice. It was even more grotesque up close—tusks jutted from its jaw, and its skin was the color of granite.

"Graah!" The creature lunged, not for Akash, but for Sanjay. Its massive hand clamped around his throat, lifting him effortlessly and slamming his head against the ceiling. The world exploded into a starburst of pain.

"Old man!" Akash's shout sounded distant.

"Where is the Divine Orb?" the Rakshasa bellowed, its foul breath washing over Sanjay's face. It threw him aside like a doll and turned on Akash, backhanding the younger man across the cabin.

As Sanjay's consciousness faded, the visions returned. Not fractured this time, but sharp, clear. A memory. His memory. A battlefield. A sword in his hand. The weight of command. The name… Aadhyan. The anger.

His eyes snapped open.

"You dare…" The voice that came from his lips was not the measured tone of a physicist. It was a low, dangerous growl. "…touch my soldier?"

With a surge of strength that wasn't his own, he was on his feet. He drove his fist into the Rakshasa's un-helmed face with the force of a battering ram. There was a sickening crunch of bone. The creature staggered back, stunned. Aadhyan—for in that moment, he was Aadhyan—grabbed the beast by its armor and hurled it through the very hole it had created. Its scream was cut short as it tumbled toward the mountains below.

Akash stared, his jaw agape. "Sir… you're back."

Aadhyan ignored him, his gaze fixed on the battlefield they were leaving behind. "Turn this barge around," he commanded, his voice ringing with absolute authority. "The battle is not over."

Their return was announced by a blast from the barge's forward cannon, which tore a hole in a cluster of advancing Rakshasa. As Aadhyan stepped onto the field, Devaj turned, a slow smile spreading across his face.

"Glad to see you're feeling more yourself, Commander."

"I…" Aadhyan began, the scientist in him fighting for control. "My memories are… fragmented. But I know my duty."

"Then your duty is to help me end this," Devaj replied, turning back to the fray.

Together, they pushed the Rakshasa line back until the enemy commander, seeing his forces decimated, threw down his sword. "We surrender!"

"Leave them," Devaj ordered his soldiers, who were ready to press the advantage. "There has been enough death today."

But as the Rakshasa commander bowed his head, his hand crept to a hidden energy pistol at his belt.

Before he could fire, a volley of searing red laser-fire erupted from the flank, cutting down the surrendering Rakshasa where they stood. The commander fell, his treacherous plan exposed as the pistol clattered to the ground.

From the smoke, another figure emerged, flanked by soldiers in polished, black armor that contrasted sharply with the battle-worn silver of Devaj's troops. The newcomer was handsome, with a cruel twist to his lips.

"I, Anshul, Crown Prince of the Amarakarsh Empire, have dealt the final blow to these traitors!" he announced, his voice dripping with arrogance.

Akash bristled. "The only Crown Prince here is Lord Devaj!"

Anshul simply sneered. "My mother rules in the Empress's stead. Her word is law. And she says that I am the heir. Enjoy your minor victory, cousin. It will be your last."

"I command all troops to stand down," Devaj said, his voice dangerously calm, cutting off Ashoka's furious retort. He turned his back on Anshul and walked away.

Later, aboard a massive transport airship heading home, the adrenaline of battle faded, leaving only a chilling unease. Aadhyan sat with Akash, watching the clouds drift by.

"Tell me about Anshul," Aadhyan said, his voice quiet again, the scientist resurfacing.

"He is the son of Lady Aarini," Akash explained, his tone low. "She was the Queen Mother's advisor. Since the Queen fell ill… Aarini has been the de facto ruler. She pushes her son as the true heir, ignoring the Prince's birthright."

"So the empire is fractured," Aadhyan murmured, piecing it together.

"More than you know, sir," Akash replied grimly. "And we're heading right into the heart of it."

Aadhyan looked ahead. In the distance, piercing the clouds, were the impossibly tall, glowing spires of a city.

"Devapuram," Akash said. "The capital. Welcome home, Commander."
