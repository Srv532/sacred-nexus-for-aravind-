The bruised, chaotic sky of the battlefield had given way to an impossible, serene blue. As their transport ship sliced silently through banks of silvery cloud, Aadhyan stood transfixed before a wide, crystalline window. Below him, the world resolved into a vision of breathtaking grandeur. It was a city that defied physics, a citadel that laughed at the laws of engineering as he, Dr. Sanjay, had known them.

Devapuram.

Enormous domes, sheathed in what looked like hammered gold, caught the sun and blazed with a light that seemed holy. Crystalline pillars, impossibly slender, soared thousands of feet into the air like spears aimed at the heavens. The entire city was cradled by a cascading series of waterfalls, originating from some unseen source high above and weaving through the urban landscape like silver veins, their mist feeding lush, emerald-green terraces at every level. At its heart, resting like a crown jewel, was a palace so vast and ornate it seemed less like a building and more like a mountain carved by the hands of gods.

Sanjay the scientist fought for purchase in Aadhyan the commander's mind. Hydroelectric power? Unlikely. The energy required to maintain this altitude, to power the lights, to levitate the stone gates we just passed… it's astronomical. It must be a centralized power source, something akin to a contained singularity, or perhaps…

"It never gets old, does it?" Akash's voice pulled him from his frantic calculations. The young soldier stood beside him, a look of profound, weary pride on his face. "Welcome to the capital, Commander. Devapuram."

"Devapuram," Aadhyan echoed, the name feeling both alien and deeply familiar on his tongue. He pressed a hand against the cool window, his gaze fixed on the central palace. "It's magnificent. Yet… it feels like a memory from someone else's life."

"Maybe all the best memories start that way," Akash offered with a wry smile. "Give it time. It's part of you, whether you remember it or not."

Aadhyan turned from the window, his expression hardening as the awe gave way to the commander's pragmatism. The beauty was a facade; every soldier knew that the most beautiful places often hid the most dangerous secrets. "Akash. Give me a true report. What is the state of the Amarakarsh Empire? The throne? The people? My instincts are telling me something is deeply wrong beneath all this glitter."

Akash's smile faded. He walked a few paces away, gripping a polished metal railing as if to steady himself. He sighed, a sound heavy with a burden too great for his youth. "It's… fractured, sir. Ever since Queen Mother Devanshi fell ill, the foundations have been cracking. She's alive, but confined to her chambers, a prisoner of her own body. No one has seen her, no one has heard her voice in almost a year."

"And the one who speaks for her?" Aadhyan pressed, already knowing the answer.

"Aarini," Akash muttered, the name leaving a bitter taste. "The Queen Mother's niece. She has royal blood, but she was never the heir. With Devanshi silenced, Aarini has wrapped herself in the mantle of authority, one decree at a time. She's a queen in all but name, presiding over a court that fears her more than it respects her."

"And Devaj? By all rights, he is the Crown Prince."

"He is," Akash affirmed, his voice fierce with loyalty. "But Aarini is a master of whispers and shadows. She paints Devaj's compassion as weakness, his honor as naivete. She uses the royal court like a weapon, passing laws and forging alliances to isolate him. Nothing is ever an overt act of treason… just a thousand tiny cuts designed to bleed him of his power before he can ever claim it."

Aadhyan found his fists were clenched, the leather of his gauntlets creaking in protest. It was an anger that felt ancient, instinctual. "How do the people stand for this?"

Akash shrugged, his shoulders slumping. "The nobles are a nest of vipers, each looking for their own advantage. Many have sided with Aarini, seeing her as the stronger horse. But the common folk, the soldiers… they haven't forgotten. They remember it was Devaj who led the charge in the border wars, who emptied the royal granaries during the Great Famine. In the streets, in the barracks, they still whisper his name. They still hope."

The airship descended with a gentle hum, passing through a final ring of levitating stones that glowed with inscriptions that pulsed with a soft, inner light. As they touched down on the grand landing platform of the citadel, a phalanx of guards in immaculate, silver-and-gold armor snapped to attention. The air here was clean and cool, carrying the scent of mountain flowers and the distant sound of chiming bells.

Beyond the guards, two women waited. The first, Tanisha, carried herself with the effortless grace of high nobility. Her deep blue robes were adorned with the silver sigils of an ancient bloodline, and her dark, intelligent eyes found Devaj's the moment he stepped from his own vessel. Her expression was a complex mixture of relief, love, and profound worry.

Beside her stood a younger woman, Aaradhya, the adopted princess of the empire. She was a radiant, striking figure, but it was her eyes that held one's attention. They were old eyes in a young face, full of a curiosity that seemed to see past the surface of things, as if she were observing a play for which she already knew the ending.

Tanisha approached, inclining her head respectfully. "Commander Aadhyan. It has been too long. Your return is a welcome light in a darkening time."

Aadhyan met her gaze, sensing a sharp political mind behind the warmth. "My lady. Forgive me, my memories are… unreliable."

Aaradhya stepped forward, a gentle, enigmatic smile on her lips. "Memories are like echoes in a canyon, Commander. They fade and return, often shaped by the landscape they find themselves in. Perhaps this land will help you remember what you need to." Her gaze was so intense it felt as though she could see the bewildered scientist hiding deep within the soldier's shell.

Devaj joined them, placing a reassuring hand on Aadhyan's shoulder. "We will unravel this, my friend. Together."

Before another word could be spoken, a long, sonorous horn blast echoed from the palace. It was not a sound of welcome; it was a demand. A royal summons.

Akash stiffened. "So soon. Aarini doesn't waste any time."

Tanisha's expression grew cold. "It is a performance. She means to display her power before the entire court."

As if on cue, a procession of heralds took their places along the grand promenade leading to the palace. But their voices rang out with a name that made the blood run cold.

"Make way for the victorious hero of the Eastern Battle! Welcome back, Crown Prince Anshul!"

The name echoed off the marble, a calculated insult. Devaj, the true commander of the battle, was not even mentioned. Aadhyan watched as courtiers and nobles, who had been waiting in the wings, rushed forward to lavish praise on Anshul, who soaked in the adulation with a preening arrogance. Devaj remained impassive, his face a mask of stoic calm, but Aadhyan could see the tightening of his jaw, the iron control he exerted over himself.

The long walk to the throne hall was a masterclass in political theater. They followed in Anshul's wake, forced to watch as he accepted the accolades that belonged to Devaj. Aadhyan's commander's mind took over, analyzing the new battlefield. He saw the subtle shifts in allegiance: a duke who once supported Devaj now bowing low to Anshul; a baroness who met Devaj's eyes with a look of pity before turning away. It was a war being fought not with swords, but with whispers, glances, and calculated omissions.

Inside the throne hall, the air was heavy with perfume and tension. Atop a crystalline throne sat Queen Aarini, a vision of regal authority in emerald robes woven with gold. Her beauty was undeniable, but her eyes were chips of ice, scanning the room with sharp, calculating precision.

"My beloved son, Anshul, returns victorious!" she announced, her voice resonating through the silent hall. She gestured for him to stand beside her, a clear statement of his position. She ignored Devaj as if he were a ghost. "He has once again proven that the strength of our empire lies in decisive action, not in misguided sentiment."

The jab was clear. Whispers rippled through the gathered nobles. Some cast scornful looks at Devaj, while others shuffled their feet, refusing to meet his gaze.

Then, Aarini's icy eyes landed on Aadhyan. "And the Commander Aadhyan. The man who forgot his own name on the battlefield. How… unfortunate. Let us hope your memory does not fail the empire as your body already has."

The insult hung in the air, sharp and cruel. Aadhyan stood silent, his face impassive, but inside, Sanjay was reeling. This was a level of political savagery he had only read about. His gaze drifted from Aarini to the towering statues of past emperors and empresses that lined the hall. As he looked at the serene, marble face of a long-dead empress, a flicker of a vision struck him. The stone lips seemed to move, and the voice he had heard in his unconsciousness echoed in his mind: Aadhyan, this is not the time for sleeping!

Was I one of them? the thought crashed through his mind. A ruler? A guardian? What am I in this world?

The audience was mercifully short, a mere formality for Aarini to cement her power. Afterwards, in a secluded palace garden away from prying eyes, Devaj finally let his mask slip.

"She grows bolder," he said to Tanisha, his voice low. "This public display was a test, to see who would flock to Anshul. She is consolidating her power."

"Her arrogance will be her undoing," Tanisha countered, her hand finding his. "The people are not so easily fooled."

Overwhelmed by the intrigue, Aadhyan wandered away from the group, his steps leading him down a long, quiet corridor lined with sculptures. It was a hall of the royal lineage. His scientist's eye, trained to notice anomalies, was immediately drawn to the central display. A magnificent sculpture of Queen Mother Devanshi stood in the middle. To her right was a sculpture of a young, noble Devaj. But to her left… was Anshul. And something was wrong.

The marble of Anshul's statue had a slightly different hue. The chisel work was cruder, lacking the masterful detail of the other two. Most damningly, its plinth was disproportionately thick, as if it were a shell built around something else.

Akash, having followed him, saw where his attention was fixed.

"Akash," Aadhyan said, his voice barely a whisper. "Why does Anshul's sculpture look like it was added as an afterthought?"

Akash went pale. He glanced nervously down the empty corridor. "Commander, I…"

"It's hiding something," Aadhyan pressed, running a hand over the clumsy seam where the new stone met the old. "It was carved over another statue. Whose?"

Akash looked trapped, his eyes wide with a fear Aadhyan hadn't seen even on the battlefield. "Sir… that… that name is not spoken anymore. By order of Queen Aarini. To even mention him is…"

"Is what, Akash? Treason?"

"Worse," Akash whispered, his voice trembling. "It's considered a curse."

Suddenly, the rhythmic marching of a guard patrol echoed from the end of the hall. Akash jumped, grabbing Aadhyan's arm.

"We must go. It is not safe to be seen here," he urged. Before turning to leave, he gave a final, desperate warning. "Forget what you saw, Commander. Some secrets are buried for a reason."

Akash hurried away, leaving Aadhyan alone in the silent hall. He turned back to the doctored statue, his mind racing. A hidden figure. A forbidden name. A secret so dangerous it was guarded by fear itself. He was no longer just a physicist lost in another world. He was a man standing at the edge of a conspiracy, and he had just found the first loose thread.

"Who," he murmured to the cold, silent stone, "are you?"
